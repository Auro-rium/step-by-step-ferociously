# FINISH Product Positioning and Language

## Product category

FINISH is a **course completion system for rigorous free online courses**.

It is not:

- a course creator
- a generic LMS
- a subscription content library
- a YouTube replacement
- a marketplace selling ownership of public videos

FINISH sells the execution layer around excellent public courses:

- an ordered course route
- saved progress
- knowledge checks
- completion gates
- a capstone brief
- a visible finish line

## Core customer

The primary customer is a computer science student or early-career engineer who:

- saves high-quality university playlists
- starts several courses but finishes few
- wants practical depth, interview readiness, or portfolio evidence
- dislikes subscriptions and huge content libraries
- needs a clear next step more than more content

## Core job to be done

> Help me complete one serious course without losing my place, wandering between resources, or finishing with nothing to show for it.

## Brand promise

**One course. One route. One finished outcome.**

## Primary message

**Stop collecting courses. Finish one.**

Supporting message:

> Follow an ordered route through excellent free lectures, prove what you learned with knowledge checks, and leave with a completed capstone.

## What users pay for

Users do not pay for the public YouTube videos. They pay for permanent access to the FINISH completion layer for a course:

- structured route
- progress history
- assessments
- capstone instructions
- completion record

Use this language explicitly wherever price is shown.

## Product vocabulary

Use these terms everywhere:

| Use | Do not use |
|---|---|
| Course | Challenge, quest |
| Course route | Quest map, learning path, journey |
| Lecture | Video unit, content item |
| Knowledge check | Checkpoint quiz, test gate |
| Final assessment | Boss quiz, final checkpoint |
| Capstone | Final task, finish project |
| Course progress | XP journey, grind progress |
| Continue course | Resume quest |
| Permanent FINISH access | Lifetime course ownership |
| Source course | Original content |

XP and streaks may remain supporting mechanics inside the product, but they must not lead sales messaging.

## Psychological model

FINISH should reduce four forms of friction:

1. **Choice friction:** one ordered route removes tab wandering and resource comparison.
2. **Memory friction:** saved position and progress remove the cost of restarting.
3. **Motivation friction:** a visible finish line and small milestones make progress concrete.
4. **Proof friction:** assessments and a capstone turn watching into evidence of learning.

The identity promise is not “be productive.” It is:

> Become someone who finishes difficult courses.

Avoid manipulative urgency, fake scarcity, fake social proof, or exaggerated credential claims.

## Product engineering priorities

1. Validate every source playlist before publication.
2. Detect missing, private, duplicated, or reordered lectures.
3. Require real course outcomes, prerequisites, difficulty, and estimated time.
4. Require authored knowledge checks tied to actual course material.
5. Keep the capstone after the complete lecture and assessment route.
6. Track funnel events from landing page through capstone completion.
7. Never promise human project review unless a real review operation exists.
8. Describe public source ownership and FINISH access honestly.

## Pricing integrity

A low one-time price can work for a self-paced route. Human project review cannot be sustainably included at the same price unless it is tightly limited or separately priced.

Recommended eventual structure:

- Self-paced completion route
- Completion route with reviewed capstone

Do not change pricing until payment operations and support costs are known.
